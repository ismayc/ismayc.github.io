Chance to finish first, week by week

Each player's chance to finish first in the pool, and take the top seed for the playoffs, through the last five seasons, from the in-season model on the daily page, run once a week on the real results.

  • The early weeks say little. Every season starts between 4% and 23% for everyone, and the favorite about 10 games in finished first (the playoffs' top seed) in 2 of 5 seasons (Jake in 2022-23, Chester in 2023-24).
  • Leaders get caught. Mike J led most of 2021-22 (83% in mid-January) and finished 2nd. Jake led 2025-26 at 90% on March 9 and finished 4th.
  • Phil finished first in 2025-26 from 0.2%. He was under 1% from early February through March 23 and passed everyone in the last two weeks.
  • Chester's 2023-24 was the steady one: the favorite every week from mid-November on.

How the simulations work

This is the model behind the daily page's chance-to-finish-first chart, run on each past season's real records once a week. Each reading comes from 100,000 simulated finishes to the season. In each one:

  1. Estimate how good each team is. Its record so far is blended with a preseason estimate: the point halfway between its Vegas line and the wins DARKO projected before the season. That estimate counts as 20 games played at its pace (49.5 wins is a .604 pace, so it adds about 12 wins and 8 losses). Early on the preseason estimate dominates; by spring the record does. (Until November 21, 2026 the estimate was the Vegas line alone and games were not played against opponents; this page was rebuilt with the current method.)
  2. Allow for doubt about that estimate. Each simulation draws the team's true win rate from a beta distribution centered on that blend, with the evidence counted at half weight. Without this step, teams given 88% chances to finish Over did so only 69% of the time over these five seasons.
  3. Play out the remaining games. Every game left on the schedule is played between its two teams: the better team's chance to win grows with the gap between the two drawn rates, and the home team gets the edge home teams have had (they won 55% of games in these five seasons). Rest and injuries are not modeled.
  4. Grade every pick and rank the entries. A team is Over if its final wins beat the line. Entries gain their wager on a right pick and lose it on a wrong one, then rank by points, number right, and 15s right. Entries still tied share the place.

A player's chance is the share of the 100,000 finishes where they end up first. Every week reuses the same random draws, so changes come from games played, not simulation noise. Readings are weekly from the day before opening night; each line stops at the last one, six days before the season ended, and the labels give each player's final place.

A caution from this run: leads at 70 to 90% held only 5 of 19 weekly readings, and players under 1% went on to finish first 4% of the time, so the model has been too sure at both ends. Weekly readings within a season are not independent, so this rests mostly on Mike J in 2021-22, Jake in 2025-26, and Phil in 2025-26.
